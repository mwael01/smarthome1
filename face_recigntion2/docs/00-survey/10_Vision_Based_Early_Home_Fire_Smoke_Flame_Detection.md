Vision-Based Early Home-Fire (Smoke & Flame) Detection
Part of: A Literature Survey on Multi-Sensor Face-Recognition-Based Smart Home Security Systems

# 1. Bibliographic Reference

YOLO-HF and related lightweight vision-based fire detection methods (2024–2025).

# 2. Problem Addressed

Traditional smoke and heat sensors have limited spatial coverage and may react only after a fire has already grown; early visual detection of smoke and small flames is desirable.

# 3. Method / Algorithm

Adapts YOLO-style single-stage object detectors with attention mechanisms and space-to-depth down-sampling to improve detection of small flames and smoke plumes in indoor scenes. Models are trained on dedicated domestic-fire datasets.

# 4. Strengths

Enables early detection from existing camera streams. Lightweight variants remain suitable for edge deployment. Complements conventional sensors rather than replacing them.

# 5. Limitations / Weaknesses

False positives under complex lighting or steam. Requires sufficient training data covering diverse indoor environments. Vision alone cannot detect fires outside the camera field of view.

# 6. Relevance to the Proposed Project

Provides the algorithmic foundation for the smoke/flame detection component of the proposed multi-sensor smart-home system.

# 7. Key Takeaway for Future Improvement

Modern object detectors can be specialised for early fire cues; fusion with simple threshold sensors improves robustness and reduces false alarms.
